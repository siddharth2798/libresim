libresim Handbook

Pilot's manual for the Cessna 172S and an A320-family airliner, over Mumbai, Pune, the Western Ghats and the coast down to Kochi. Procedures below are the keystrokes the sim uses. F2 opens the same lists in the cockpit.

Part GEN · Before you fly

GEN 1Overview

Region Aircraft What both share

libresim is a local program. There is no account and no server. The only network use is optional: a live weather report, and the text-to-speech already on your computer.

The region

  • Detailed area. About 145 × 115 km around Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Thane creek, the Western Ghats and Pune. Terrain is 30 m, imagery 10 m, with 3D buildings from OpenStreetMap.
  • Outer ring. 90 m terrain and 80 m imagery out to about 245 km: Nashik, Ahmednagar, Satara, Kolhapur and the Konkan coast.
  • Corridor. A coastal strip to Kochi at 30 m terrain and 20 m imagery, with Goa, Mangaluru, Kannur, Kozhikode and Kochi, and 13 more airfields.
  • Beyond. Out to about 490 km the terrain is filled from the nearest real data, so the world has no edge.

The two aircraft

Both are chosen on the start screen and from --aircraft=. Training lessons always use the Cessna.

C172The Skyhawk SP uses the FlightGear c172p model and cockpit, and a flight model checked against the Pilot's Operating Handbook. Full numbers and how to fly it are in OPS 2.

A320The airliner uses the FlightGear A320-family model and data (GPL-2.0). It is not an Airbus product and it is not affiliated with Airbus. It has a normal law, FADEC, an FCU, a lite MCDU and glass displays. What is still simplified is in OPS 3. The working list is docs/A320-STATUS.md.

What both share

  • The same world: runways and taxiways from OpenStreetMap, stands, follow-me, pushback, ground vehicles, night lighting, and landmark terminals.
  • Weather from a preset or, if you turn it on, a live METAR plus a forecast. Ridge lift, thermals, storms, rain and icing act on the aircraft.
  • A radio with a controller that follows the flight, checklists, failures, saves, a 10-minute replay, and video recording.
  • Paint. Each aircraft has its own colours and sticker slots. Wings on the airliner stay white.

GEN 2Install and run

Quick start Requirements Packages

Quick start

In the project folder, double-click Start libresim.desktop (Linux) or Start libresim.command (macOS). The first launch sets the machine up, asks before any sudo, then opens the game. Later launches just open it. On GNOME, right-click the file once and choose Allow Launching.

From a terminal:

git clone <repository> libresim && cd libresim
./run.sh
  • The first run prints a plan: downloads in GB, which pipeline steps will run, and how long to expect. You confirm before it starts. A full first run is hours (about 2.5 GB; imagery alone can take 30–60 minutes). Later runs start in seconds.
  • A step is skipped when its outputs are already newer than its scripts. Editing a comment does not rebuild anything.
  • Progress is a count, a size, and a heartbeat every 15 seconds. The log is data/run.log.
  • Missing packages are detected for apt, dnf, pacman and Homebrew. Godot 4.7.2 is offered into ~/.local/bin if it is missing. Blender is not needed to fly.
  • Game options pass straight through: ./run.sh --airport=VABB --stand.
run.sh
OptionEffect
--setup-onlyBuild, and do not launch
--skip-dataLaunch even if the world data is incomplete
--region=Rmumbai_pune or all (the default, which also fetches the corridor)
--a320Also fetch the FlightGear A320-family data (about 2.5 MB) and regenerate its flight-model files. This does not download a separate aircraft pack
--force-step=NNRe-run one pipeline step. Repeatable
--dry-runPrint the plan and change nothing
--yes, -yAnswer yes to the plan, sudo and the Godot download

The two aircraft you fly are already in the repository. --a320 is only for regenerating the airliner's flight-model JSON from upstream. A withdrawable archive of just that aircraft is tools/package/make_pack.sh.

After the data pipeline has run, from the repo root (needs the Godot 4.7.2 export templates):

The macOS build is ad-hoc signed and not notarised. The first time, right-click the app and choose Open, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/libresim.app. Joysticks on macOS need two environment variables; run.sh, Start libresim.command and a packaged .app set them. If you launch Godot yourself:

Command-line options work in every package: ./libresim.sh -- --airport=VABB --stand. The AppImage takes them directly. On a read-only install the renderer toggle cannot write override.cfg; start with --rendering-method forward_plus instead.

GEN 3Start a flight

Home Free flight Livery Airport Weather The other steps Options and controls

After the splash, the home screen asks what you want to do. Free flight and Training are the two cards. 1 / 2 or the arrow keys move, Enter confirms. Handbook, Credits and Quit sit in the top bar.

Home

  • Fly again (R) starts your last free-flight setup, after the same check as Fly now.
  • Continue (C) starts the next training lesson, with full guidance. The card shows each lesson's stars. Training is the Cessna only: if the airliner was selected, the lesson switches to the C172.
  • Saved flights, Controls and Options are the links under the cards.

Free flight

The left rail is the order of the flight, and the aircraft comes first because the airport page depends on it:

  1. Aircraft. Specs, registration, fuel and payload.
  2. Livery. Optional. Next skips it.
  3. Airport and start.
  4. Weather and time.
  5. Failures. Optional.
  6. Recording. Optional.

Each row shows its current value and a mark: green ready, amber look at this, red a problem, hollow optional. Fly now is in the bottom bar from any step. It turns amber Fly anyway when the setup has a problem. Keys 1–6 jump (not while you are typing), Page Up / Page Down step through, Ctrl+Enter flies, Esc goes back. The last setup is user://last_flight.json.

Fly anywayFly asks, and never blocks, when you are over max takeoff weight, the centre of gravity is outside the envelope, the runway is shorter than the aircraft's minimum (runway starts only), the tailwind is above 5 kt, or the crosswind is above the aircraft's limit (C172 15 kt, A320 33 kt). Gusts do not count. Wind is checked for runway and air starts, not for a stand. Visibility under 1.6 km is only a warning. Go back has the focus. Lessons and saved flights skip the question.

Aircraft and the call sign

  • The list is every game/aircraft/<id>/aircraft.json. Today that is the Cessna 172S and the A320-family airliner.
  • Registration is painted on the aircraft. Left alone, it follows the aircraft: VT-LBR or VT-ZZA. A typed one (3 to 7 letters or digits, one optional dash, such as VT-ABC or N172SP) stays when you change aircraft. --callsign= sets it too. On the Cessna that registration is also the radio call. On the airliner the radio stays Skyline 123 unless what you typed is itself an airline call, such as Skyline 456. The same field is under Options → Radio / ATC. VT-FSM is only the fallback when neither you nor the aircraft names one.
  • C172Fuel per tank (default 17 US gal each), front seats, rear seats and baggage (up to 54 kg). The pilot is 81.6 kg. The chart is the normal and utility envelope in inches and pounds. Landing assumes 5 gal a tank.
  • A320Fuel is one total in kilograms (wing tanks first, then the centre tank). Cabin and cargo are separate. The chart is kilograms and percent of mean aerodynamic chord, and it is marked approximate.

Livery

Paint is per aircraft and is remembered with the setup. Presets stay on this computer, under user://liveries/.

  • C172Body, main accent, pin stripe, and struts. Two picture slots: fuselage side and fin.
  • A320Fuselage, fin, cheat line and engines. Wings, slats, flaps, spoilers and the tailplane stay the factory white. Picture slots: fuselage side and fin.

A picture can be PNG, JPG or WebP, up to 4 MB. It appears on the aircraft as you place it. You can change its size, slide it sideways and up, and mirror it to the other side. Reset returns the factory paint and removes pictures. --livery= paints a command-line flight and does not save.

Airport and start

  • Search by ICAO, name or city. Every word must match. Airports that are too short for the aircraft are dimmed.
  • The diagram is north-up. Click a runway end or a stand.
  • Runway. Engine running (both engines, on the airliner). One chip per end shows length and the wind, and turns red when Fly would ask. Tick cold and dark to start with everything off.
  • Stand or gate. Always cold and dark, parking brake set. Real OpenStreetMap refs are kept. A stand without one becomes Gate n or Bay n. Lists put gates first for the airliner and bays or tie-downs first for the Cessna.
  • In the air. Height and distance start at that aircraft's approach and follow the aircraft until you edit one. Default puts them back. Speed has no slider: it is the aircraft's own, unless --air-kt or a lesson sets it.

Weather and time

Presets run from Clear to Monsoon. The day strip is a 24-hour bar; click or drag for quarter hours. Dawn and Dusk are the real sunrise and sunset for the month you pick, for the middle of the map (18.75 N, 73.4 E), not for each airport. The wind dial sets direction in 10° steps and draws the chosen runway. The headwind and crosswind line follows that aircraft's limit.

More weather opens cloud, base, visibility, rain, turbulence, thermals, storm cells, icing, gusts, QNH, temperature, clock rate and month. Live weather is the switch on this page. It is off until you turn it on. Details are in OPS 6.

Failures, recording, and the wait

Failures: pick an area, arm one, and choose a trigger (at start, after a time, above an altitude, or above an airspeed). Random failures hide which ones are armed. "Tell me when something fails" is optional. The areas follow the aircraft. The lists are in OPS 7.

Recording: tick Record this flight, then the folder, 720p, 1080p or native, and 24, 30 or 60 fps. F8 still records in flight if you left the tick off. Files are libresim_<date>_<time>.mkv. With ffmpeg installed, an MP4 is made when you stop.

Opening a flight, a lesson, a save, or the menu plays a short film: Mumbai on an evening final for the airliner, the Juhu coast for a Cessna at Juhu or Mumbai, and the Ghats otherwise. It stays up until the ground and the runway are in, at most 12 seconds. A short wait, such as painting a livery or reading the weather, uses a plain veil. Live weather animates its status line while it fetches.

Options and controls

Controls (also J in flight) has Keyboard, Joystick and gamepad, and Other keys. Controls for lists the other aircraft's actions without changing your setup. Search is Ctrl+F. Keyboard slots take up to three keys. A joystick axis has Tune: invert, range, deadzone, curve. A latching switch has its own Invert, and the live bar shows the state the sim acts on. The Captain Pack is below, under OPS 1.

Options has six tabs and saves at once: Sound, Graphics, Display and units, Flight controls, Radio / ATC, and Training aids. UI size is Auto, or 100, 125, 150 or 200 percent of the system scale. The 3D view stays full resolution. Radio assist (on by default) tunes COM1 for you and suggests the next call. Voice uses the system speech synthesiser and is off until you enable it. On Linux install speech-dispatcher and a voice such as espeak-ng, then restart.

Saved flights are cards. Fly from this save resumes there. Delete asks first. Make one with Esc → Save flight, or F11.

Command lineFlight options skip this screen and use defaults, not your last setup. Add --menu to open the screen already filled in. The full list is REF 1.

GEN 4C172Training

Three short lessons at Pune, in calm clear weather. They do not change your free-flight setup. Start them from the Training card or with --lesson=takeoff, cruise or landing, and optionally ,full, ,light or ,off. An airliner selection is switched to the Cessna for the lesson. Lessons do not fetch live weather.

LessonYou practise
TakeoffFull power, the centreline, rotate at 55 kt, climb at 74 kt, reach 500 ft
CruiseLevel flight, cruise power, trim, a 90° turn, a 500 ft climb and descent
LandingSlowing, flaps, the 3° path, the threshold, the flare, the roll-out

The landing lesson starts 3 nm out at 1,000 ft and 80 kt, flaps up, on the path. Slow to about 1,700 RPM and 58–78 kt, take flaps 20 and about 65 kt, stay within 1.5° of the path and be stable by 0.8 nm, cross the threshold near 60 kt, flare, and brake to 10 kt or less.

  • The card on the left lists the steps. Gauges go green, amber or red against the target. Markers draw the centreline, glide-path rings, an aim box, and a turn beacon in the cruise lesson.
  • Help is Full guidance, Hints only, or Minimal. Stars come from the graded numbers. You can try again, take the next lesson, keep flying, or return to the menu.
  • If the lesson can no longer be finished it ends with what happened and what to work on. Try again and F5 put you back at the start.

ATC stays quiet in a lesson unless you call. Callouts are on for Full guidance and Hints only, even if you left them off. Minimal leaves them as you set them.

OPS 1Controls, views and pause

Keys Feel Joystick and the Captain Pack Views Pause and messages

The same stick, keys and views fly both aircraft. What a control does depends on which aircraft is loaded: mixture and magnetos exist on the Cessna, engine masters and the gear lever exist on the airliner. The full key list is REF 2. F1 shows it in flight.

Flying with the keyboard

  • ↓ pulls the nose up, ↑ pushes it down. ← → roll. Z X yaw, and steer the nosewheel.
  • Page Up / Page Down move the throttle (both levers together on the airliner). Home / End trim nose down / nose up.
  • ] or F7 extends one flap notch, [ or F6 retracts one. On the airliner the notches are 0, 1, 1+F, 2, 3 and FULL, and the slats move first.
  • B holds both brakes, , . the pedals, Shift+B the parking brake. Y toggles the mouse yoke.
  • Rebind anything on Controls → Keyboard. Keys match by physical position, so a label change does not move them. Up to three keys per action.

Control feel

Options → Flight controls, three choices:

  • Yoke keys. Spring (the default) re-centres when you let go. Sticky leaves the controls where you put them.
  • Smoothing. Off, light (the default) or strong, on keyboard, joystick and mouse yoke.
  • Auto-coordination. Rudder follows aileron. Use it if you have no pedals.

Joystick, gamepad and the Captain Pack

Connected devices are listed and hot-plug works. Bind moves an axis or presses a button. A bound axis takes over from the keyboard; unbound actions stay on the keys. The generic gamepad uses the left stick for roll and pitch, the right stick for yaw, the triggers as toe brakes, the shoulder buttons for flaps, and the face buttons for brakes, parking brake and views.

If a Thrustmaster TCA Captain Pack is plugged in, the start screen offers its profile once. Set the switch on the back of the sidestick to PC. Plug the quadrant into the computer, not into the stick. On macOS, move each lever once after launch or it stays unread. The gear lever is stored inverted, because the hardware reads pressed as up. Any latching switch has an Invert toggle on its row. The sidestick's change-view button is the next camera. One profile serves both aircraft: the flaps lever's notches mean 0–30° on the Cessna and 0, 1, 2, 3, FULL on the airliner, and notch 1 on the ground is 1+F. Linux button numbers for this hardware have not been verified; press Bind and the page records what it sees.

Cameras

C cycles, Shift+C goes back, and 1–8 pick cockpit, chase, orbit, free, tower, wing, tail and fly-by. Right-drag looks around. In the cockpit, the wheel, a trackpad scroll, or a pinch zooms. Point at a lever or switch and a note shows its name, the key that moves it, and where it is set. The same wheel sets orbit distance or free-camera speed. V re-centres. Ctrl+click zooms to the instrument or switch under the cursor. A left-drag on empty cockpit space looks around; a left-click on a screen or an airliner lever still uses that control.

Outside views can show a 2D panel. Tab cycles Full, Split, Compact, Minimal and Off. The choice is remembered. The chase camera is sized to the aircraft and does not zoom when speed changes.

Pause, messages, and the crash card

Esc pauses on a full screen. The flight stops, and the engine, wind, cockpit, radio and spoken callouts go quiet until you resume. Menu volume is left as you set it. The left column is Resume, Options, Weather and time (F10), Controls (J), Save / Load (F11), Failures (F9), Checklists (F2), Instant replay (R), Restart (F5), Quit to the menu, and Quit. Esc backs out one level, then resumes. Restart and the two Quit entries need a second click.

Cards at the top centre report failures, damage and the landing debrief. Blue, amber, red. Repeats merge. At most three show. A stall is a pulsing banner. After a crash the card names the cause and offers Restart, Watch replay, or New flight.

Cold start

A stand start, a runway start with Cold and dark ticked, and --cold begin here. Parking brake is set. Battery, alternator, avionics and lights are off. Mixture is idle cutoff, throttle closed, magnetos OFF. Fuel is already on BOTH and the shutoff is open. The altimeter reads 29.92 inHg, which is 1013 hPa, not whatever QNH you picked. A runway start without Cold and dark skips this: the engine is running and the altimeter is already set.

The switches are the bottom row of the panel, or these keys. Levers move while you hold the key, about half their travel per second.

  1. Shift+M. Battery and alternator on. The starter does not turn on a dead battery.
  2. Hold ' for about two seconds. Mixture full rich. ; is lean, all the way to idle cutoff.
  3. Tap Page Up. Throttle cracked, not open. Page Down closes it.
  4. M three times. Magnetos go OFF, then L, then R, then BOTH. One more M from BOTH is OFF again.
  5. Ctrl+B. Beacon on.
  6. Hold S. It cranks, catches after about a second, and idles when you release S.
  7. Oil pressure comes off zero. Shift+V turns the avionics, and the radios, on.
  8. Set the altimeter. Click either half of the Kollsman knob, 0.01 inHg a click, Shift for 0.10. 0.01 inHg is about 0.34 hPa.

If it will not fire: battery on, mixture rich, magnetos BOTH, fuel selector not OFF, shutoff pushed in, then hold S. Shift+F cycles the selector OFF, LEFT, RIGHT, BOTH. Ctrl+F toggles the shutoff.

Taxi and takeoff

  1. Parking brake stays set (Shift+B toggles it). Heading indicator: middle-click its knob to match the compass. Flaps 0° or 10°: ] is one notch down, [ is up. Notches are 0, 10, 20, 30 and take a few seconds.
  2. Shift+B to release the brake. T draws the taxi route to the into-wind runway and sends the follow-me; T again clears it. Steer with Z and X. Hold B to brake. , and . are the toe brakes.
  3. At the runway: Ctrl+S strobes, Ctrl+L landing light. Page Up to full throttle, about 2,300 RPM. Hold right rudder (X). The aeroplane pulls left.
  4. At 55 kt, ease ↓ and rotate. Climb at 74 kt. In the climb, hold a little right aileron as well. Above 500 ft, flaps up if you used 10°.
  5. Level off, trim with Home (nose down) and End (nose up) until it holds, then about 2,400 RPM. For cruise, lean with ;. Hands-off after a minute is about 10° left at 90 KIAS and about level at 100. Up through 110 KIAS the bank stays inside about 10°.

From Juhu (VAJJ) the Ghats are about 45 km east. Pune (VAPO) is about 120 km east-southeast.

Landing and shutdown

  1. Mixture rich (hold '). Fuel BOTH. Ctrl+L landing light.
  2. Flaps 10° only below 110 kt. More flaps only below 85 kt. Full is 30°.
  3. 65–75 kt with flaps up, 60–70 kt with flaps down. Land on the mains, then the nosewheel. Brake with B.
  4. Clear of the runway: flaps up, strobes off (Ctrl+S), landing light off.

Callouts are off until you press K. Takeoff: airspeed alive at 40 kt, rotate at 55, positive rate. Approach: the heights on the way down. After you stop, the debrief grades the landing. Limits are in OPS 8.

To shut down, parked: Shift+B brake set, throttle idle, Shift+V avionics off, hold ; to idle cutoff, M to magnetos OFF, Shift+M master off.

The panel

Six-pack, tachometer, engine and fuel cluster, and a wet compass. It is on the 3D panel in the cockpit and as a 2D strip outside (Tab).

Click the left or right half of a knob, or use the wheel:

The instruments are not perfect on purpose. Airspeed reads about 6 kt low near the stall. The vertical speed takes about 6 seconds to settle. The attitude gyro erects slowly and shows small turn and acceleration errors; after a vacuum failure it spins down and flags. The heading indicator drifts about 4° per 15 minutes. The compass has the usual northerly turning error. The turn coordinator is electric.

The switch row is BAT, ALT, avionics, lights, pitot heat, fuel shutoff and alternate static. The magneto dial reads OFF, R, L, BOTH. The M key cycles OFF, L, R, BOTH. Fuel is OFF / LEFT / RIGHT / BOTH. Keyboard equivalents are in REF 2. Alternate static has no key until you bind one on the Controls page.

The radio stack

Two KX-155 COM/NAV radios, KR-87 ADF, KT-76A transponder and a DME that follows NAV1. The wheel over a standby frequency tunes it (COM 25 kHz, NAV 50 kHz, ADF 1 kHz; Shift is the coarse step). The swap arrow trades active and standby. The transponder wheel changes a digit 0–7, and the mode box cycles OFF, SBY, ON, ALT. Defaults: COM1 118.10 / 121.90, NAV1 116.60 / 113.90, NAV2 108.10 / 109.30, ADF 381, squawk 7000. The avionics master has to be on.

NAV, the map and ATC are shared with the airliner and are in OPS 4.

OPS 3A320A320-family

What is modelled Speeds How it flies Cold start Taxi and takeoff Landing Flight deck A route

What is modelled

Two FADEC turbofans, slats and flaps, spoilers, a trimmable stabiliser, retractable gear, nosewheel steering, anti-skid and autobrake. Displays: PFD, ND, engine and warning, systems, FCU, lite MCDU, on the 3D panel and on the Full and Split cards outside.

Not modelled: a published SID or STAR, constraint altitudes, predicted VNAV, a bleed or pack system, or an overhead that matches the real aeroplane. A route can follow a published Indian airway between two fixes that lie on it. Sounds are stand-ins. F2 is the checklist.

NameThe screen says "A320-family twin-jet airliner". Airbus and A320 are trademarks of Airbus. libresim is not affiliated with or endorsed by Airbus.

Speeds

Callout speeds in the sim are fixed: airspeed alive 80 kt, V1 135, rotate 140. They do not change with weight. Approach is about 135 KIAS and does change with weight. VMO / MMO are 350 KIAS / M 0.82. The data set's design dive speed is 380 KIAS. Fly anyway asks above 33 kt of crosswind. A runway shorter than about 1,800 m is dimmed, and it is never the default.

1 g stall speeds in this data set, near 64 t: clean 155 kt, CONF 1 about 129, 1+F about 122, CONF 2 about 115, CONF 3 about 113, FULL about 109. The speed tape shows the limit for the flap lever where it is. Load factor beyond about +2.5 / −1.0 g is the airframe limit.

How it flies

In normal law the sidestick asks for a load factor in pitch and a roll rate in roll. Let go in roll and it holds the bank. Pitch trim follows you. Protections cover angle of attack, high speed, bank, pitch and load factor. A failure can drop the law to alternate or direct. Below about 50 ft the flare law holds the attitude you have set. On the ground the spoilers stay out through a bounce, and the slats lead the flaps when you move the lever.

Keyboard pitch and roll are the sidestick. Page Up and Page Down move both thrust levers. Alt+Page Up / Page Down are engine 1 alone, Alt+Home / End engine 2. Alt+= / − step the detent. Reverse is Ctrl+X or Num *. Speedbrake out is \, in is /. Gear is Ctrl+G. Autobrake cycles with Ctrl+A.

Cold start

A stand or gate, and a runway start with Cold and dark ticked, begin here. Batteries are already on, so the displays work. There is no battery key. Engines are off, masters off, engine mode NORM, thrust at idle, parking brake set, APU off, lights off. A runway start without Cold and dark has both engines running. Skip to taxi.

The engines start on battery air. The APU is optional.

  1. APU, if you want it: Ctrl+U master on, then hold Alt+U for a moment. The start latches. It is available in about 30 seconds. Ctrl+U again shuts it down. There is no overhead sequence beyond those two keys.
  2. Ctrl+I twice. Engine mode goes NORM, then CRANK, then IGN/START. Leave it on IGN/START. CRANK only motors an engine whose master is off, and does not light it. One more Ctrl+I from IGN/START is NORM.
  3. Ctrl+D. Engine 2 master on. You do not hold it. N2 rises on its own. Light-off is about 20 seconds. Idle is about 45 seconds, near 19% N1 and 59% N2.
  4. Ctrl+E. Engine 1 master on. Same wait. Either engine can be first.
  5. Ctrl+I once, back to NORM. APU off with Ctrl+U if you no longer need it.

If an engine does not light: mode is IGN/START, that master is on, and there is fuel. A failed engine or an engine fire will not start. The same controls are the knob and the master levers on the pedestal. They show the position you set.

Taxi and takeoff

  1. Flaps for takeoff: ] once is CONF 1, slats out and flaps still in, on the ground and in the air. ] again is 1+F. A third press is CONF 2. [ retracts one detent. On the Captain Pack the first notch is 1+F on the ground and CONF 1 in the air.
  2. Ctrl+A cycles autobrake OFF, LO, MED, MAX. Use MAX for takeoff. Speedbrake in with /. \ walks it out. Ctrl+B beacon, Ctrl+N nav lights.
  3. Shift+B releases the parking brake. At a gate, P pushes back and swings the tail toward the taxiway, then stops before a wheel leaves the pavement. The brake has to be off. P again stops. T is the taxi route and the follow-me. Stay under 30 kt.
  4. Steer with Z and X. That is the rudder pedals, about 6° of nosewheel, fading out by about 130 kt. A tiller axis on a joystick steers further and fades by about 70 kt. The Controls page lists it: bind the axis, or give Tiller left and Tiller right a key. Ctrl+T is the taxi light.
  5. Lined up: Ctrl+S strobes, Ctrl+L landing lights. Alt+= steps both thrust levers IDLE, CL, FLX/MCT, TOGA. Takeoff is FLX/MCT or TOGA. Page Up moves both levers smoothly. Alt+Page Up / Page Down is engine 1. Alt+Home / End is engine 2.
  6. Callouts, if K is on: airspeed alive 80, V1 135, rotate 140. These three do not change with weight. Rotate with ↓. Gear up is Ctrl+G. Retract flaps with [. Autobrake off with Ctrl+A.

An air start is already set up: 7 nm out, 2,200 ft above the field, 160 KIAS, gear down, CONF 2. --air-kt at 200 or more raises the gear and cleans up.

Landing and parking

  1. Ctrl+L landing lights. Ctrl+G gear down. Ctrl+A to LO or MED.
  2. Flaps: from clean, ] four times is CONF 3 and five times is FULL. The checklist allows CONF 3. Stabilise near the approach speed on the tape, about 135 kt and it does change with weight.
  3. Leave the speedbrake lever in. Ground spoilers come out on their own above about 30 kt with both levers at idle or in reverse, and they stay out through a bounce until a lever goes above idle. Reverse: from idle, hold Ctrl+X or Num *. Release it and the levers return to idle.
  4. Vacate: speedbrake in, flaps up, landing lights and strobes off. Ctrl+U then Alt+U if you want the APU for the gate.
  5. Parked: Shift+B brake set, Ctrl+E and Ctrl+D masters off, beacon off.

At a jetway the bridge reaches the L1 door in about 6 seconds once you are parked within 3 m, and retracts when you leave. It is not solid while it is extended. Limits are in OPS 8.

The flight deck

  • PFD. Attitude, speed tape with the flap limit, altitude, heading, flight-mode annunciations.
  • ND. ARC and ROSE. Weather radar paints on it when there is a return, marked WX. There is no VOR, ILS or PLAN mode.
  • E/WD and SD. Engine, fuel, wheel and flight-control pages. ICE DETECTED and ANTI ICE show on the memo line. There is no schematic flight-control page, and no bleed or pack model.
  • FCU. The knobs are clickable. From the keyboard: Ctrl+1 AP1, Ctrl+2 AP2, Ctrl+3 autothrust, Ctrl+4 LOC, Ctrl+5 APPR. Alt and the arrows move heading and altitude. Alt+[ / ] move the speed. Alt+' / ; move vertical speed. A sidestick movement takes the aeroplane back from the autopilot.

Lights beyond the usual set: Alt+T take-off, Alt+R runway turn-off, Alt+W wing and engine scan, Alt+O logo. Wing anti-ice is Ctrl+W. Engine anti-ice is Alt+E and Alt+D. Probe heat is automatic. Anti-ice uses bleed air and costs a little thrust.

A route

The MCDU is on the pedestal, and on the Full and Split cards outside. Scratchpad keys work when the pointer is over that unit.

  • INIT FROM/TO files a route: VABB/BBB/VAPO is origin, optional vias, destination. An ident that is not an airport, a VOR, an NDB, a published airway fix or a synthetic runway fix is rejected.
  • A designator between two fixes that lie on it expands to the published points. VABB/BBB/W56/BIA/VOBL flies W56 from BBB to BIA, from the Airports Authority of India eAIP. The entry and the exit have to be on that airway.
  • DEP RWY and ARR RWY insert a fix 5 nm ahead of the departure runway and a fix 8 nm out on the arrival centreline (VABB27D, VAPO10F). They are not a SID or a STAR.
  • F-PLN edits a leg. DIR skips to a waypoint already in the plan or inserts one and then resumes it.
  • Filing or a direct-to selects NAV. The heading window shows dashes. Middle-click the heading knob to go back to a selected heading. With the autopilot on, NAV flies the leg and sequences within 1 nm.
  • Cruise flight level on INIT is copied to the FCU altitude and selects managed vertical. At top of descent the FCU altitude is lowered to the destination elevation plus 3,000 ft. There are no constraint altitudes.

The same route can be built on the moving map, which both aircraft share. That is OPS 4.

OPS 4Navigation and the radio

Radio navaids ILS Map and GPS Weather radar ATC Checklists

VOR, NDB and DME

Navaids come from OurAirports. Range depends on altitude and on terrain line of sight, checked twice a second. Low behind the Western Ghats, you lose the Pune VOR. The receivers need avionics power. On the Cessna you tune them on the stack. On the airliner the route uses the same database through the MCDU and the map.

ILS, simulated

There is no real ILS record in the airport data, so localisers and 3° glideslopes are generated for runways of 2,000 m and longer. The geometry follows the runway. The frequency and the ident do not. The map labels them simulated. The airliner's LOC and APPR modes fly this signal.

The moving map

N opens it, centred on you, about 20 nm across, wider if a route needs the room. Imagery, airports, runways, VOR and NDB, ILS feathers, your track, and the weather-radar sweep when there is weather to paint.

  • Click an airport or a navaid for a direct-to. Shift-click adds a leg. On the airliner this is the same route the MCDU files.
  • The wheel zooms around the cursor, from 0.3 to 300 nm. While the map is open, = and − zoom instead of moving the throttle. Page Up and Page Down still do.
  • Drag to pan. Space follows again. O swaps north-up and heading-up. G fits the route. Backspace clears it.

Weather radar

On the airliner's ND, and on the moving map for either aircraft, the radar is a nose sweep: plus or minus 60°, a 3° beam, one colour per cell. Green, then amber, then red as the return strengthens. Dry cloud, including a dry overcast, stays black. Rain is a ragged field. A red cell blacks out the rest of that radial. Clear air is blank. The ND marks WX when it has a return.

ATC

Press ` or click the bar at the top left. The bar shows COM1, who would answer, and the suggested next call. The list is at most four plain calls. Arrows move, Enter or 1–4 sends, A is say again, H is the last 30 transmissions, V is the voice. The controller's words also arrive as a card.

Radio assist is on by default. A call tunes COM1 and the bar suggests the next call. Off, you tune COM1 yourself, nothing is suggested, and the list offers Tune COM1 first when you are on the wrong frequency. Airports with no published frequency answer on any COM1 frequency. Small fields have no ATC: your calls are blind. A dead COM1 sends nothing.

C172The flight is VFR. Parked: ATIS, clearance, then pushback and taxi. At the holding point, Tower. After landing: vacate, Ground, taxi to parking. Taking off or landing without a clearance draws one note per flight. It does not stop you.

A320You are Skyline 123, a medium aircraft, on an IFR-style phrase set. There is no SID, no STAR, no speed instruction and no wake separation. The route you fly is the one you filed, not one ATC assigns.

Phraseology follows ICAO and Indian AIP practice. The text uses written numbers. The voice, if you turn it on, speaks them the ICAO way. In a lesson the controller says nothing unless you call, and the bar is hidden.

Checklists

F2 opens the checklist for the aircraft you are flying. Items the sim can see tick themselves. The others tick on click or Enter. Ctrl+Page Up / Page Down change list. The window docks on the right the first time. Drag the title, resize from the edges, and use the opacity slider if you want the background to drop out. The position is remembered. It hides while you are paused.

T draws a taxi route to the into-wind runway and names the taxiways. The follow-me car drives ahead and peels off at the hold-short point. T again clears it. P pushes back, with the parking brake off, swings the tail toward the taxiway, and stops before a wheel leaves the pavement. P again stops. Backspace dismisses the message. The route line stays in the radio bar.

Lighting

At night, within 25 km of an airport: runway edge lights (the last 600 m amber on an instrument runway), threshold and end bars, centreline and touchdown-zone lights on ILS runways, sequenced approach lights on ILS ends, a PAPI to the left of each runway end (two red and two white on a 3° path, visible by day), blue taxiway edges, green centreline, and stop bars when it is dark or the visibility is under 5 km. PAPIs, the follow-me and the tug are not solid. Masts, poles, buildings and parked vehicles are. A jetway is solid while it is retracted, and is not solid while it is extended.

Apron floodlights cover every stand of a lit airport, on from just before sunset. The few nearest banks are real lights. The rest are a pool of light on the ground. A mast is never left standing in a taxi corridor.

Your own lights: nav, beacon, strobes, landing and taxi. At night or in dusk a runway start turns on nav, beacon, strobes, landing and taxi. An air start below 3,000 ft adds the landing light. Cold and dark, and every stand start, leaves them off. By day, only the beacon. The airliner also has retractable landing lights, take-off and runway-turnoff lights, logo lights, and wing and engine scan lights. The panel dimmer is L / Shift+L.

Live weather

Off unless you turn it on, on the Weather step or with --live-weather. Lessons ignore it.

  • One METAR from aviationweather.gov, from a station within 50 km, plus an Open-Meteo forecast for the winds aloft and for anything the METAR does not carry. Pune has no METAR, so it uses the model alone.
  • The first time, a panel tells you that a station id, or coordinates rounded to about a kilometre, will be sent. Nothing else about you is.
  • No connection: the last fetch (user://live_weather.json), then the preset you had selected.
  • While it is on, the presets and sliders are read-only. Use as base copies the numbers into the sliders and turns live weather off.
  • Update during flight is off. On, it fetches every 15 minutes and eases the change in over about 5 minutes. The altimeter setting you have already dialled is not moved.
  • The METAR is added to the radio history. ATIS includes the dew point when one was reported. The attribution line gains the two weather sources.

Wind, cloud and rain

  • The surface wind strengthens with height, then blends toward the wind aloft. Gusts are on top. The Western Ghats give ridge lift on the windward side, on the order of 3 m/s in a 20 kt westerly, and sink and turbulence on the lee side.
  • Thermals and storm cells are their own sliders. Thermals run about 1–3 m/s under cumulus by day, stronger in the middle of the layer, none over a cold sea or under a thick deck. Storm cells, in monsoon cloud, can put a downdraft of about 10 m/s in the core and a shear near the ground. A card says wind shear.
  • Inside cloud, cockpit visibility falls to about 100 m in the dense parts. Outside views keep the aircraft visible. Rain falls only below the tops, beads on the windshield, and darkens the ground. Heavy rain lengthens a braked stop, to about 1.8 times the dry distance at full rain. Below a rain setting of 0.3 it comes and goes in showers.
  • QNH and temperature change the density altitude. The Kollsman window starts at the QNH you chose.

Icing

Ice forms in cloud between about +2 °C and −20 °C. On a 30 °C day in this region that band is roughly FL150 to FL250, so the airliner can ice there and the Cessna almost never does. The icing slider is how much supercooled water the cloud holds. Presets set it; Monsoon is the highest. Storm cells add their own share.

A full load of airframe ice adds drag and raises the stall speed. Inlet ice costs power or thrust. Pitot ice and static ice behave like the blocked-port failures until the ice falls off. Alternate static bypasses an iced static port.

C172Pitot heat, with the battery bus on, is the protection. There is no wing de-ice.

A320Wing and engine anti-ice run from engine bleed and cost a little thrust. Probes heat on their own. The warning display shows ICE DETECTED until anti-ice is on.

Sun, moon and night

The sun is the real sun for this map. The default flight is 10:00 IST on 14 February. F10 can run the clock faster. The moon has the phase and the place for the date, including a glitter path on the water. City glow sits on the horizon. Night exposure is lifted so the coast and the cloud decks stay readable.

OPS 7Systems and failures

Cessna systems Airliner systems Failures

Cessna

  • Electrical. A 24 V, 35 Ah battery and a 60 A alternator. Shift+M switches the battery and the alternator together. The alternator needs roughly 1,000 RPM, so the ammeter discharges at idle. Low volts comes on below 24.5 V. The starter needs the battery. Flaps and the turn coordinator need the bus.
  • Fuel. Left and right, as the selector says. After starvation the engine runs about six seconds on the line, and restarts while windmilling if fuel returns.
  • Vacuum drives the attitude and heading gyros. Pitot and static drive the airspeed, altimeter and vertical speed. Alternate static is on the switch row.

Airliner

  • Electrical. Two engine generators, the APU generator and the batteries. The piston alternator failure is not on this aircraft.
  • Hydraulics. Green, blue and yellow. A failed system is a failure you can arm.
  • Fuel is in kilograms, with pumps for the left, centre and right tanks. The pipe tanks are not a separate feed model: the main tanks feed together.
  • Engines. N1, N2, EGT, fuel flow, reverse and a start. No oil system and no bleed model beyond the anti-ice penalty. An engine fire is a failure, not a modelled fire handle.
  • Magnetos, the vacuum pump and the steam gyros are not on this aircraft. Air data is three reference failures instead.

Arm them on the start screen or with --fail=. In flight, F9 shows every system as OK, failed or armed, and can fail or repair one immediately. Random failures pick inside the window you set (default 2–30 minutes) and do not tell you which. --random-failures arms one.

C172A prop strike destroys the engine, and above about 25 kt the aircraft noses over. A wingtip strike above 10 kt is a crash. A tail strike damages the airframe. Ditching is a crash. More than a couple of seconds above VNE damages the airframe. The test cards put the takeoff roll near 865 ft against a POH figure of 960 ft, and a full-flap braked landing roll near 610 ft against 575 ft.

A320There is no propeller to strike. Ground spoilers hold through a bounce. The flare law is already in from about 50 ft. Tyre bursts, bird strikes, and engine damage from temperature or RPM are not modelled on either aircraft.

What you can hit

Touching a solid with any part of the airframe ends the flight, in the air or on the ground. The card names the part and the thing. Solids are trees as drawn, 3D buildings, landmark terminals, retracted jetways, parked vehicles, floodlight masts and windsock poles. You can taxi under an 8 m canopy. You cannot taxi through a wall.

Runway lights, PAPIs, the follow-me and the pushback tug are not solid. An extended jetway is not solid either. Stands, the pushback swing, taxiways and runways forgive buildings and trees that the map put in the way, and an overlap that exists while you are parked is forgiven until you drive away. Terminals, retracted jetways and parked vehicles stay solid.

Weight and balance

The flight model uses the weight and the centre of gravity you loaded. An aft centre of gravity lightens the pitch. The chart on the aircraft page turns red when you are outside the envelope, and Fly anyway asks before you go. The Cessna envelope is the handbook's normal and utility. The airliner chart is an approximate public shape, in percent of mean aerodynamic chord, and the page says so.

OPS 9Saves, replay and recording

  • Quick-save is F11. Shift+F11 loads it. Esc → Save / Load is the named list. A save keeps the aircraft, the place, the motion, the engines, the fuel, the damage, the switches, the radios, the failures, the weather, the time and the paint.
  • Replay (R) is the last 10 minutes, from any camera. Space plays, the arrows seek 5 seconds, [ ] change speed from 0.25× to 4×, and you can scrub the timeline. Take control resumes from that moment. Exit returns you to where you were. Replay does not repaint the aircraft.
  • Recording (F8, or the tick on the start screen) writes an MKV with picture and sound, playable in VLC and mpv even if the game is killed. About 5 MB/s at 720p30. ffmpeg, if installed, makes an MP4 when you stop. Read-only installs write under the Godot user folder.
  • F5 restarts at the original start, including the start of a lesson, and puts the cockpit controls back to what that start uses, a dragged tiller included. An airborne restart also tunes COM1 to approach, or to the tower when the airport has no approach.

Saves and settings live in Godot's user folder. On Linux that is ~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/libresim/. A command-line flight, a screenshot run and a test do not write your last_flight.json or your options.

The engine will not start

C172Shift+M, hold ' about two seconds, tap Page Up, M three times, hold S. The full order is in OPS 2. A dead battery means no starter.

A320Ctrl+I twice so the mode is IGN/START, then Ctrl+D and Ctrl+E, and wait about 45 seconds. The full order is in OPS 3.

The Cessna keeps rolling left

That is the solo weight and the engine. It is about neutral at 100 KIAS in level flight. In the climb, hold a little right aileron and rudder. See OPS 2.

ATC does not answer

COM1 has to be the frequency of the unit you called, and the avionics have to be powered. With radio assist on, the call tunes it. With assist off, pick Tune COM1 in the radio list. Fields with no published frequency answer on any frequency. A training lesson stays silent until you call.

Is the ILS real?

The path is the runway's. The frequency and the ident are generated. The map says simulated.

Can I fly online, or with today's weather?

There is no multiplayer and no live traffic. Live weather is optional, on the Weather step and with --live-weather. It sends a station id or a coarse position, and nothing else. See OPS 6.

Where are my files?

In the Godot user folder: last_flight.json, key and joystick bindings, options.cfg, audio.cfg, graphics.cfg, saves/ and liveries/. On Linux, ~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/libresim/.

What is not in the sim yet?

A Cessna autopilot, spoken ATC with real radio sounds, other traffic in the circuit, and a fuller airliner: published SIDs and STARs, constraint altitudes, and VNAV predictions. The route you can file today already follows published Indian airways.
