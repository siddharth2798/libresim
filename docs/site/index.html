libresim Handbook

libresim is a free, open-source desktop flight simulator of the Cessna 172S over Mumbai, Pune and the Western Ghats, with a coastal corridor running south to Kochi. This handbook covers flying it first, then building and changing it.

Part GEN · General

GEN 1Overview

libresim is a local, offline flight simulator built around one aircraft and one region, done in depth. It runs as a native desktop program on Linux, with a macOS build also produced. There are no accounts, servers or online services.

1.1The region

  • Detailed area: about 145 × 115 km around Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, the Thane creek, the Western Ghats (Lonavala, Khandala, Sinhagad) and Pune. It has 30 m terrain and 10 m Sentinel-2 imagery, 3D buildings from 578k OpenStreetMap footprints, water and forests.
  • Outer ring: 90 m terrain and 80 m imagery out to about 245 km, reaching Nashik, Ahmednagar, Satara, Kolhapur and the Konkan coast.
  • Konkan–Kerala corridor (phase R): a coastal strip to Kochi with 30 m terrain and 20 m imagery, covering Goa, Mangaluru, Kannur, Kozhikode and Kochi, with 13 more airfields.
  • Beyond: terrain to about 490 km is filled from the nearest real data with generated relief, so the world has no edge.

1.2The aircraft

The Cessna 172S Skyhawk SP: the FlightGear c172p 3D model and cockpit, flown by a JSBSim-derived six-degree-of-freedom flight model with libresim's own engine, propeller and gear code. Headless test cards check it against the C172S Pilot's Operating Handbook (POH).

1.3Highlights

  • Real terrain and satellite imagery everywhere, runways and taxiways from OpenStreetMap, 3D buildings, animated water, volumetric clouds and forests.
  • A working steam-gauge panel with modelled instrument errors, two NAV/COMs, ADF, DME, transponder, a GPS direct-to line and a moving map.
  • Electrical, fuel, vacuum and pitot-static systems with 26 schedulable failures and random failures.
  • Weather presets from Clear to Monsoon, ridge lift off the Ghats, rain on the windshield, a real sun and moon, and ICAO/FAA aerodrome lighting at night.
  • Airport operations: stand and gate starts, taxi routing with a follow-me car, pushback, ground vehicles, ATC that follows your flight from clearance to parking, and auto-ticking checklists.
  • A damage model: hard landings, gear collapse, prop strikes, ditching, overspeed and overstress.
  • Saved flights, a 10-minute instant replay, and built-in video recording.
  • Real recorded sounds throughout.

GEN 2Install and run

2.1Quick start from source

No terminal needed. In the project folder, double-click Start libresim.desktop (Linux) or Start libresim.command (macOS). When everything is already set up the game simply opens. The first time, a window opens, sets everything up (asking before any sudo install) and then starts the game. On GNOME, right-click the file once and choose Allow Launching. Every launch also adds libresim, with its logo, to your applications menu, so you can start it from there next time.

From a terminal, one script checks prerequisites, downloads the raw data, builds the game data and opens the game:

git clone <repository> libresim && cd libresim
./run.sh
  • The first run takes about 1–2 hours, mostly downloads. Each step runs only when its outputs are missing or older than its scripts or inputs (stamps live in data/.stamps/), so later runs start the game in seconds.
  • Missing system packages (GDAL, python3-venv, curl, git, unzip) are detected for apt, dnf, pacman and Homebrew. The script prints the install command and runs it with sudo only after you say yes.
  • If Godot 4.7.2 is missing, it offers to download it into ~/.local/bin. Blender is not needed.
  • Everything is logged to data/run.log.
  • Game options pass straight through: ./run.sh --airport=VABB --stand.

GEN 3Start screen

Running the game opens the start screen, after the libresim splash. It asks one question first, What would you like to do?, with two large cards: Free flight (pick the airport, aircraft, weather and failures) and Training (guided lessons: take off, cruise, land). Click a card, or press 1 / 2 (or ← / →) and Enter; the card you chose last time is focused. Small buttons below open Saved flights, Controls and Options, each with a ◀ Back button (or Esc) to the home screen.

Free flight is a guided stepper: Airport → Aircraft → Weather → Failures → Recording → Fly. The current step is highlighted, finished steps get a tick, and every pill is clickable. Next: <step> ▶ moves on, ◀ Back returns (on the first step, to the home screen), and on the last step the filled button is FLY ▶. Keys 1–5 jump to a step, Page Up / Page Down step through and Ctrl+Enter flies. It remembers your last setup (user://last_flight.json). A summary line at the bottom shows the whole setup, for example VAJJ · runway (longest) · Fair · 10:00 IST · wind 270/8 · 0 failure(s). Training shows only the lessons, the help level and a START LESSON ▶ button, without the stepper.

3.1Airport and start position

  • Pick an airport from the searchable list. A north-up diagram shows its runways, taxiways and stands, with an arrow at your start point.
  • Runway: the chosen runway end with the engine running. Tick "cold & dark" to start with everything off.
  • Stand / gate: a stand from OpenStreetMap (jetbridge gate, remote bay or GA parking). Stand starts are always cold and dark with the parking brake set, as in the real aircraft.
  • In the air: a height above the field and a distance on final to the chosen runway. The aircraft starts trimmed for the actual weather and wind.

3.2Aircraft, payload and weight and balance

  • Every game/aircraft/<id>/aircraft.json is listed with its specifications. Today that is the Cessna 172S.
  • Set fuel per tank (default 17 US gal each) and the front-seat, rear-seat and baggage loads (baggage up to 54 kg). The pilot is 81.6 kg (180 lb).
  • The takeoff weight is checked against the 1157 kg (2550 lb) MTOW.
  • A weight and balance chart plots the C172S normal and utility CG envelopes with your takeoff and landing points (landing assumes 5 gal per tank). It turns red when overweight or out of CG limits.
  • The flight model flies the CG you load: an aft CG makes the pitch light and less stable.

3.3Weather and time

  • Presets: Clear, Fair, Scattered, Broken (passing showers), Overcast (drizzle), Winter haze, Monsoon (heavy rain under a low deck, occasional lightning). Values are in OPS 5.
  • Custom sliders: cloud cover, cloud base, visibility, rain, turbulence, QNH, temperature.
  • Time of day: presets or a slider, plus the clock rate and the month. Dawn and Dusk are the real sunrise and sunset for the chosen month (mid-February 07:10 and 18:40 IST; June about 06:00 and 19:15).
  • Wind: presets, direction, speed and gusts, and a "90° crosswind" button. A live head/crosswind readout for the chosen runway turns red above the C172's 15 kt demonstrated crosswind, or with a tailwind.

3.4Failures

  • Any of the 26 failures (catalogue in OPS 6.5) can be scheduled with a trigger: at start, after N min, above N ft AGL or above N kt.
  • Random failures: choose how many, a time window (default 2–30 min) and which systems may fail. You are not told which failures are armed.
  • "Tell me when something fails" shows a notice card. With it off, diagnose the failure from the instruments and the aircraft's behaviour.

3.5Recording

Tick "Record this flight" to record the whole session, picture and sound. Choose the folder, 720p, 1080p or native resolution, and 24, 30 or 60 fps. Details are in OPS 8.3.

3.6Controls

Joystick, gamepad and keyboard bindings, also reachable in flight with J. See OPS 1.3 and OPS 1.4. The page also lists the fixed hotkeys.

3.7Options

  • Volume and mute for each sound bus: Master, Engine, Wind & environment, Cockpit, Radio, Menus.
  • Graphics preset.
  • Flight-control feel: yoke keys, input smoothing, auto-coordination (OPS 1.5).
  • Units preference (stored for later; the C172 panel stays in ft, kt and inHg).
  • Whether the flight-data bar shows at start.
  • Voice, both off by default: "Speak them" for training callouts and "ATC voice" for the radio transcript. They use your operating system's text-to-speech. If no voice is found the tick stays saved but silent, and a short note appears under it. On Linux install speech-dispatcher and a voice such as espeak-ng (for example sudo apt install speech-dispatcher espeak-ng), then restart the game; Windows and macOS use their built-in voices.

Saved in user://audio.cfg and user://options.cfg.

3.8Saved flights

Saved situations with a thumbnail, airport, altitude and IAS. "Fly from this save" resumes exactly there.

NoteCommand-line flight options skip the start screen and start from the defaults, not from your last setup. Add --menu to open the start screen pre-filled with them instead.

GEN 4Training mode

Three short, separate lessons teach the basics. Choose Training on the start screen's home screen (or run --lesson=takeoff, cruise or landing, optionally followed by ,full, ,light or ,off). Lessons start at Pune in calm, clear weather, and your free-flight setup is not changed.

LessonYou practise
TakeoffFull power, staying on the centreline, rotating at 55 kt, climbing at VY (74 kt), reaching 500 ft
CruiseLevel flight, cruise power, trim, a 90° turn, a 500 ft climb and a 500 ft descent
LandingSlowing down, flaps, the 3° glide path and centreline, the threshold, the flare, touchdown and roll-out

The landing lesson starts 3 nm out on final (1,000 ft, 80 kt, flaps up, on the 3° path), so there is time to set up. Its steps: Slow down (power about 1,700 RPM, 58 to 78 kt), Flaps and trim (flaps 20° with ] or F7 twice and about 65 kt; End trims nose-up, Home nose-down), Fly the glide path (within 1.5° of the path, stable by 0.8 nm out), Cross the threshold (about 60 kt), Flare and touch down and Roll out (brake with B to walking pace, 10 kt ground speed or less).

4.1What helps you

  • The lesson card (left) lists the steps, shows the current instruction and a hint, and ticks steps off as you complete them.
  • Live gauges show your value against the target and its tolerance: green inside, amber near, red far.
  • 3D markers: a dashed line down the runway centreline; glide-path rings on the approach (cyan ahead, yellow next, green flown, red missed); an aim-point box on the runway; a beacon to turn toward in the cruise lesson.
  • Coach messages warn you as you go: "Drifting off the centreline", "Rotate now!", "High. Reduce power, lower the nose". If "Speak them" is on, your system's voice says them too. Training callouts (OPS 1.11) are always on during a lesson.

4.2How much help

LevelWhat you get
Full guidanceStep card, hints, live gauges, 3D markers and callouts
Hints onlyStep card, hints and callouts; no gauges or markers
MinimalJust the step list, graded at the end, like a check ride

4.3Results

When the last step is done, a card shows 1–3 stars and each graded number (for example centreline error, rotation speed, climb speed, sink rate at touchdown, touchdown point and roll-out straightness) marked Good, OK or Needs work. Choose Try again, Next lesson, Keep flying or Menu. Your best stars are remembered. Lessons are data (game/training/lessons.json): steps, conditions, gauges, grades and abort rules, so you can add your own.

4.4When a lesson cannot be finished

Steps are in order, but not strict: if you already meet a later step's conditions (for example you are configured and stable on the glide path before you finished "Slow down"), the earlier steps are ticked off for you.

A lesson never leaves you stuck on a step. If you can no longer finish it (you touched down or left the runway before the lesson expected it, got far too low or too high on the approach, floated off the end of the runway, settled back onto the runway after rotating, flew into the ground, or crashed), it ends with a "not completed" card: what happened and in which step, which steps you did finish, what to work on, and Try again, Keep flying or Menu. After a crash the lesson card says so, and F5 (or Restart on the crash card) starts the lesson again. Try again and F5 put the aircraft back at the lesson start whatever state it was in: airborne, stopped on the runway or crashed.

1.4Rebinding keyboard keys

  • Click a key slot (up to 3 keys per action, with Shift, Ctrl or Alt allowed), then press the new key. Esc cancels; Delete clears the slot.
  • Conflicts with other actions or with fixed hotkeys show in orange.
  • Keys match by physical position, so the layout works on any keyboard language.
  • Saved in user://key_bindings.json.

1.7Cold-and-dark start-up

Stand starts and --cold begin with everything off and the parking brake set. Runway and air starts have the engine running. Press F2 to follow the POH checklists; most items tick themselves when the sim sees them done.

  1. Parking brake set, fuel selector BOTH, fuel shutoff valve ON, avionics master OFF (switch row, bottom of the panel).
  2. Throttle open about 1/4 inch: a short press of PgUp.
  3. Mixture RICH: hold '.
  4. Master switch BAT and ALT on: Shift+M or click them on the switch row. Beacon on: Ctrl+B.
  5. Magnetos BOTH: press M until the key shows BOTH.
  6. Hold S until the engine catches, then release. The starter needs battery power.
  7. Oil pressure in the green within 30 s; avionics master on; ammeter charging; set the altimeter to QNH.
  8. Release the parking brake with Shift+B before taxiing, or press P for pushback.

The alternator needs more than about 1000 RPM, so at idle the ammeter shows a discharge.

1.8Takeoff and climb

A first flight from Juhu:

  1. PgUp to full throttle (about 2300 RPM static).
  2. Keep straight with X: P-factor and torque turn the aircraft left on the roll.
  3. At 55 kt, hold ↓ gently to rotate.
  4. Climb at 74 kt (VY). Expect to hold a little right aileron and right rudder at climb power.
  5. Trim with Home/End until the aircraft flies level hands-off, then reduce to about 2400 RPM for cruise.

The Ghats are 45 km east of Juhu; Pune (VAPO) is about 120 km east-southeast.

1.9Trim and the left-wing-heavy drift

Pitch trim slews at 0.15 per second while the key is held; a trim axis is also supported. There is no cockpit aileron or rudder trim: fixed rigging tabs (aileron 0.033, rudder 0.004) cancel the left roll from the solo pilot's weight and engine torque at about 100 KIAS. Slower or at climb power the aircraft rolls gently left; faster it rolls slightly right. Hands off from trim, the bank after 60 s is about −10° at 90 KIAS, 0° at 100 KIAS and +10° at 110 KIAS, which matches JSBSim's c172p.

1.10Approach and landing

  • Slow below 110 kt before flaps 10°, and below 85 kt before more. Flaps take a few seconds to travel.
  • On final with flaps 30°, about 61 KIAS over the threshold gives a POH-like landing. The PAPI shows two red and two white lights on a 3° path (OPS 4.6).
  • In a crosswind, crab or fly wing-low: touching down while drifting sideways more than about 6 kt damages the gear.
  • Flare to a gentle sink rate. Landing nose wheel first risks a prop strike.

1.11Training callouts (optional)

A real C172 has no automatic callouts, so they are off by default. Press K in flight, or tick "Training callouts" on the Options page, to hear and see what an instructor would say. "Speak them" uses your system's text-to-speech voice if it has one (on Linux: speech-dispatcher plus a voice such as espeak-ng); otherwise the calls show as large text above the hint line.

  • Takeoff: Airspeed alive (40 kt), Rotate (55 kt), Positive rate.
  • Approach: Five hundred, One hundred, Fifty, Thirty, Twenty, Ten (feet above ground, once each, only while descending).
  • Cautions: Sink rate (over 1000 fpm below 500 ft), Low airspeed (under 54 kt below 400 ft), Bank angle (over 35° below 500 ft).

1.12On-screen messages and the crash card

  • Notification cards at the top centre carry failures, damage and the landing debrief. Blue is info, amber caution, red warning. Repeats merge ("×3"), at most three show at once, and they fade after a few seconds.
  • STALL is a pulsing banner. A one-line hint at the bottom says when the engine has stopped.
  • Crash card: after a crash a centred card dims the scene and gives the reason, the impact numbers and the damage, with buttons to Restart (F5), Watch replay (R) or start a New flight.

OPS 2Cockpit and instruments

The panel is a C172S steam-gauge layout drawn onto the real FlightGear c172p panel at each instrument's true position. It shows on the 3D panel in the cockpit view and as a 2D strip at the bottom of the screen in outside views.

2.1What is on the panel

  • Six-pack: airspeed, attitude, altimeter, turn coordinator, heading indicator, VSI.
  • Tachometer.
  • Engine and fuel cluster: fuel L/R, oil temperature and pressure, EGT, fuel flow, vacuum, ammeter.
  • Wet compass strip.
  • Right-hand column: VOR1 (KI-208 CDI) on NAV1, VOR2/ILS (KI-209 with glideslope) on NAV2, ADF (KI-227).
  • Radio stack: a GPS line, two KX-155 COM/NAVs, KR-87 ADF, KT-76A transponder, KI-266 DME (follows NAV1).
  • Switch row (bottom of the panel).

3.3ATC

In free flight a slim radio bar sits at the top-left, level with the flight-data bar. It shows COM1 and the unit you are tuned to (COM1 118.100 · Pune Tower: green when a unit answers on that frequency, amber "no facility", red when COM1 is off or failed), the call to make next (Next: ask Ground for taxi) and, for 20 seconds, the latest controller message. Click the bar or press ` (backquote) to open a short list of at most four plain calls, the suggested one first and highlighted. ↑ ↓ move, Enter or 1–4 make the call, Esc or ` close, and the list closes by itself after a call. While it is open the arrow keys do not pitch the aircraft and the number keys do not change the cameras. The small row under the list has Say again (A), History (H, the last 30 transmissions, scrollable) and Voice on/off (V).

What the controller says arrives as one calm card at the top-centre, like the other notification cards, and fades after 6 to 8 seconds; your own call and read-back appear in it as small muted lines. The controller also calls on its own (handovers, "report final", "vacate").

  • A call is answered only by the right unit. With assist off, a call to the wrong unit of the same airport is redirected ("contact Tower 118.1") and a call on a frequency with no unit gets no reply; the radio says which frequency to tune.
  • Say again repeats the last instruction. Pilot readbacks are automatic and ATC answers "readback correct" to a clearance.
  • Airports with frequencies in the data (Mumbai, Pune, Goa, Cochin, Calicut, Mangaluru, Belgaum, Kolhapur, Garuda) need the right frequency; a unit that does not exist is covered by the next one (Pune has only a tower, which does everything). Airports without frequencies in the data (Juhu, Navi Mumbai and similar) answer on any frequency. Small fields and heliports have no ATC: your calls are blind transmissions that nobody answers.
  • With COM1 failed or no avionics power nothing is sent or heard (a failure also says: squawk 7600, lost-communications procedure).
  • Never blocking: take off, land or enter a runway without a clearance and you get one short note, once per flight, and the flight carries on.
  • Training lessons: ATC stays quiet. No calls, no notes, the radio bar stays hidden, and clearances count as given. The radio is still there on `.
  • Optional voice: V in the open radio (or atc_voice in options.cfg). It uses your operating system's text-to-speech voices and is off by default. Recorded voices and radio effects come later.

4.4Pushback

Release the parking brake, then press P. A Goldhofer tug pushes you back and swings the tail toward the taxiway. P again stops it.

4.5Ground vehicles

  • At jetway gates: a ground power unit at the nose, a baggage tug with carts aft, and a fuel truck on some stands. The jetways are the FlightGear model laid along the OSM jetbridge.
  • Elsewhere: fuel trucks at some GA and remote stands, apron buses, and a fire truck by the tower.
  • Service vehicles are parked; moving service vehicles and AI traffic are not modelled yet.

4.6Aerodrome lighting

Lighting follows ICAO/FAA patterns and is built for each airport within 25 km. Lights are directional, fade in haze and dim by day.

  • Runway: white edge lights (last 600 m yellow on instrument runways), green threshold and red end bars with wing bars, centreline lights (white, then red/white alternating 900–300 m from the end, red for the last 300 m), touchdown-zone barrettes on ILS runways.
  • Approach: ALSF-II-style lights with sequenced flashers ("the rabbit") on ILS ends; a 420 m simple system elsewhere. By day the flashers are dimmed.
  • PAPI on the left of each runway, visible by day too.
  • Taxiways: blue edge and green centreline lights, with green/yellow lead-on lights.
  • Holding points: red stop bars and flashing runway guard lights at night and when visibility is under 5 km.
  • Rotating green/white aerodrome beacons and apron floodlight masts.

4.7Landmark terminals

The passenger terminals at three airports are dedicated models built from their OSM footprints and a hand-written recipe per airport, instead of generic extruded blocks. Glass curtain walls reflect the sky and glow warm at night. Each loads when the camera comes within 20 km.

5.2Wind, turbulence and ridge lift

  • A logarithmic surface wind profile blends into the wind aloft, which is veered about 10° and about 1.6 × the surface speed.
  • Gusts and turbulence on top.
  • Ridge lift on windward slopes: the Ghats give about 3 m/s in a 20 kt westerly. The lee side has sink and mechanical turbulence.
  • QNH and temperature drive density altitude and the altimeter. Pune afternoons are hot and high (VAPO is at 1942 ft).

5.3Clouds, visibility and rain

  • Clouds are a raymarched volumetric cumulus layer at the set base and cover. You can fly under, through (fog inside) and above it. It drifts with the wind aloft and is lit by the sun, with darker bases and warm sunset tints.
  • Visibility drives haze and fog: everything fades to the horizon colour with an extinction of 3 / visibility (5 % contrast at the visibility distance).
  • Broken and thicker cover dims and diffuses the sun. Under Overcast there are no hard shadows or sun disc. Climb above the tops and the sun comes back.
  • Rain falls only below the cloud tops. Streaks lean with the wind and stretch with airspeed; drops bead on the windshield when parked and streak back in flight; terrain, roofs and trees turn darker and glossier, with standing water on flat ground that dries slowly. Rain lowers visibility and sounds louder on the canopy at speed. Below 0.3 it comes and goes in showers.
  • Monsoon storms have occasional lightning.

5.4Sun, moon and time

  • The sun position is real for Mumbai and Pune in IST. The default is 10:00 on 14 February; the F10 time rate speeds the clock up.
  • In February, 06:30 is still pre-dawn twilight; sunrise is 07:10 and sunset 18:40.
  • The moon has the correct position and phase for the date, with cool moonlight and a glitter path on water. --day=3 gives a full moon.

5.5Night

  • Stars, a warm city glow on the horizon and cloud undersides, and a night ambient level and exposure so terrain, coastlines and clouds stay readable.
  • Building facades get procedural windows that light up at night.
  • Aircraft lights: red/green/white nav lights, a flashing red fin beacon and double-flash white wingtip strobes are drawn as glowing points with a minimum on-screen size, so the aircraft stays visible from the tower miles away. Small shadowless lights tint the wingtips, fin and wings around them, and a soft fill keeps the airframe readable in outside views.
  • Landing and taxi lights are spot lights with a faint visible beam and a lit pool on the runway ahead, fading out above about 80 m AGL. In daylight only the bulbs glow.
  • Lights at start (night or dusk, sun below 5°): nav and beacon always; strobes for air and runway starts; landing light for runway starts and air starts below 3000 ft AGL; taxi light for runway starts. Cold-and-dark and stand starts have everything off. By day only the beacon is on.
  • Cockpit at night: the instruments are internally lit and follow the PANEL dimmer (unpowered means dark), the radio displays stay lit, and a warm glareshield flood lights the panel and yokes. The daytime look is unchanged.
  • Aerodrome lighting is described in OPS 4.6.

OPS 6Systems and failures

6.1Electrical

  • 24 V battery (35 Ah) and 60 A alternator; BAT and ALT masters and an avionics master.
  • The alternator needs more than about 1000 RPM, so the ammeter shows discharge at idle.
  • The LOW VOLTS light comes on below 24.5 V.
  • The starter needs battery power; the flaps, turn coordinator and radios need bus power.

6.2Fuel

  • Left and right tanks feed according to the selector (OFF / LEFT / RIGHT / BOTH), through a shutoff valve.
  • After starvation the engine runs about 6 s on fuel in the lines, and restarts while windmilling once fuel returns.
  • Fuel burn changes weight and CG.

6.3Vacuum

The engine-driven vacuum pump spins the attitude and heading gyros. After a pump failure they spin down slowly and wander; the attitude indicator shows a GYRO flag.

6.4Pitot-static

A blocked pitot or static port freezes that pressure at the moment of failure, so the airspeed, altimeter and VSI read wrong the way they would in the real aircraft. The ALT STAT switch selects the alternate static source; PITOT HT is on the switch row.

What counts as a crash

Touching a solid ends the flight at any speed. The whole airframe is tested, not a few points: the propeller disc, nose, cowling, fuselage, both wings with their struts, the tail fin and stabilizers, and the wheels, so a 6 m house or a single tree between a wingtip and the nose is hit by the wing. The crash card, and the replay, say which part hit what: “The nose hit a parked vehicle (fuel truck) at 6 kt (taxiing)”.

  • Solids: the trees you see (canopy and height), 3D OSM buildings, the landmark terminals at Mumbai, Pune and Kochi (their real shape: an 8 m canopy leaves room to taxi underneath, a wall does not), jetways, parked ground vehicles, apron floodlight masts and windsock poles. Runway and approach lights, PAPIs, the follow-me car and the pushback tug are not solid.
  • No height gate: a tall building beside a cliff is hit however far the ground is below you. Building collision data loads around the aircraft and where it is heading, not around the camera.
  • Normal operations never crash: stands, the pushback swing, taxiways and runways are corridors where OSM buildings and trees (not survey-grade data) are forgiven, ground vehicles and masts that would stand in them are left out, and an overlap that exists while you sit parked (a stand beside a tree) is forgiven until you drive away. Terminals, jetways and vehicles stay solid.

Not modelled yet: tyre bursts, engine damage from over-temperature or over-speed, bird strikes.

7.4Weight and balance

The CG envelope comes from the C172S POH (section 2 limits, section 6 chart), plotted in inches aft of the datum (front face of the firewall) against weight in lb. The loads you set change the mass, CG and inertia in the flight model. With only the pilot aboard, the CG sits slightly left of centre (180 lb pilot 14 in left), which together with engine torque gives the slow left roll described in OPS 1.9.

DEV 2For developers

2.1Architecture

  • Property tree: all sim state lives in a flat Props autoload with paths like fdm/cas_mps, systems/electrical/bus_volts, controls/com1/freq_mhz or failures/vacuum_pump. Modules talk only through properties, which keeps them small and independent.
  • FDM at 120 Hz in _physics_process, decoupled from rendering. Rendering interpolates the state (in 64-bit) every frame.
  • Floating origin: the FloatingOrigin autoload shifts the world every 5 km to keep the camera near (0, 0, 0). Render position = Vector3(e − ox, up, −(n − oy)).
  • Terrain: a quadtree of streamed heightmap tiles with LOD selection, geomorphing and LRU eviction.
  • SI units inside; conversion only at instruments and UI (core/units.gd).
  • Aircraft as data: behaviour lives in JSON under game/aircraft/c172/, not in code.

The engine won't start

Check BAT on, mixture rich ('), throttle cracked, magnetos BOTH, fuel selector not OFF and shutoff valve on, then hold S. A dead battery or a battery failure means no starter.

The aircraft keeps rolling left

That is the real C172 flown solo: pilot weight and engine torque. It is neutral at about 100 KIAS in level flight; at climb power hold a little right aileron and rudder (OPS 1.9).

ATC doesn't answer

COM1's active frequency must match the unit you call. With radio assist on (the default) a call tunes it for you; with assist off open the radio with ` and pick Tune COM1 to …, or tune the standby and click ⇄. The avionics must be powered. Airports without frequencies in the data answer on any frequency; small fields and heliports have no ATC. In a training lesson ATC says nothing unless you call.

I lost the Pune VOR

Reception checks terrain line of sight; climb or move clear of the Ghats.

Is the ILS real?

No. The localizer and glideslope geometry follow real runways, but frequencies and idents are generated (OPS 3.2).

Can I fly online or with live weather?

No. libresim is offline by design: no multiplayer, live traffic or live weather.

Where are my settings and saves?

In Godot's user folder (user://, on Linux ~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/libresim/): last_flight.json, key_bindings.json, input_bindings.json, graphics.cfg, audio.cfg, options.cfg and saves/.
